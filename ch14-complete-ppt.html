Topic 01 — Measuring Athletic Performance

Low-Speed vs High-Speed Strength

Low-Speed Strength
Force a muscle/group exerts in one maximal effort.
Quantified by 1RM (bench, squat) or max isokinetic force.
No expensive equipment needed → most common max-strength test.
Why it matters1RM tests reflect the dynamic ability sport requires.
High-Speed Strength
Ability to exert high force at high contraction speed.
Also called (max) anaerobic power.
≈1 s duration → ATP-PCr is primary energy source.
Scores includePower clean/snatch 1RM, vertical jump height, stair-sprint time.
Relative strength = load lifted (or force, N) ÷ body weight. Ratio scaling assumes strength ∝ body mass — allometric scaling (massexponent) is used when body size varies widely.
Topic 01 — Measuring Athletic Performance

High-Speed Power & Net Impulse

Jump Height = Net Impulse
Jump height is a function of force applied > bodyweight over time (net impulse) during ankle/knee/hip extension.
↑ net impulse (force × time) → ↑ takeoff velocity.
Interpretation trapAthlete gains body mass but jumps to the same height → same takeoff velocity on a heavier body = power output ↑, even though jump height looks unchanged.

Cycle ergometer power tests (e.g. Wingate) suit injury situations where running is restricted, or non-bodyweight-support sports like rowing/cycling.
Topic 01 — Measuring Athletic Performance

Load-Velocity Profile (VBT)

Velocity-Based Training
Bar speed ↓ linearly as load ↑ → individualized profile from loads at 20/40/60/80/90% 1RM.
Useful for prescribing/evaluating training intensity.
Do not use to predict 1RM. Estimating 1RM from a load-velocity profile is highly variable and consistently overestimates true 1RM — risks mis-programmed, potentially unsafe loads. 1RM should be directly tested.

Anaerobic Capacity & Muscular Endurance

Anaerobic Capacity
Max rate of energy production, phosphagen + glycolytic systems combined.
Quantified as max power output over 30–90 s.
(vs. anaerobic power tests: only a few seconds).
Local Muscular Endurance
Certain muscle(s) performing repeated contractions vs submaximal resistance.
Continuous, no rest, no extraneous movement.
e.g. max chin-ups, dips, push-ups, or reps at % 1RM.

Aerobic Capacity

Aerobic Power
Max rate of energy production via oxidation of CHO/fat/protein.
Expressed as mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹ (VO₂max).
Direct measurement uncommon in the field → estimated via performance tests.

Athletic Motor Skill Competency

AMSC (Lloyd et al.)
Foundational movement skills underpinning athletic movement in youth.
Screened via tools: FMS, tuck jump assessment (TJA), RTSB, back squat assessment.
Process-oriented assessmentIdentifies strength/mobility/neuromuscular deficits to guide programming.
Record on video for retrospective review. Best-repetition scoring (FMS, RTSB) vs sum-of-repetitions scoring (TJA) — the latter captures movement variability that youth show due to ongoing brain maturation and growth spurts.

Flexibility, Balance & Stability

Flexibility
Range of motion about a joint.
Goniometer (joint angle) or sit-and-reach box (low back/hip).
Move slowly into stretch, hold — no ballistic bouncing allowed.
Balance & Stability
Balance = maintaining COG over base of support.
Stability = returning to position after disturbance.
BESS and SEBT: strong reliability, well supported in literature.

Body Composition & Anthropometry

Body Composition
Relative proportions of fat vs lean mass (2-component model).
Skinfold calipers: most valid/reliable field method (r = 0.99).
DXA = gold standard; bioimpedance/plethysmography also used.
Anthropometry
Height, weight, selected girths.
Stadiometer for height; certified balance scale for weight.
Weigh in the morning, minimal dry clothing, consistent hydration.

Topic 03 — Selected Test Protocols

1RM Tests I

1RM Bench Press
EquipmentBarbell, plates, 2 safety locks, 5 lb gradations; adjustable bench.
PersonnelOne spotter, one recorder.
1. Warm up 5–10 reps light-moderate load.
2. 2+ heavier warm-up sets of 2–5 reps.
3. Find 1RM within 3–5 attempts post warm-up.
1RM Back Squat
EquipmentBarbell/plates/locks; squat rack with spotting bars; flat solid surface.
PersonnelTwo spotters, one recorder.
1. Warm-up as per bench press.
2. Load increments are larger than bench press.
3. 1RM within 3–5 attempts.
Topic 03 — Selected Test Protocols

1RM Tests II

1RM Power Clean
EquipmentOlympic barbell (revolving sleeve), plates, locks; dedicated lifting platform.
PersonnelOne tester/recorder.
1. Warm-up/load increments as per bench press.
2. High technical demand — two athletes with equal power can post very different 1RMs.
3. 1RM within 3–5 attempts.
1RM Bench Pull
EquipmentBarbell/plates/locks; high flat (seal row) bench.
PersonnelOne spotter, one recorder.
1. Closed pronated grip, wider than shoulders, from the hang position.
2. Bar to lower chest/upper abdomen, elbows up; feet stay off the ground.
3. Valid rep = bar touches bench + full elbow extension on return.
Topic 03 — Selected Test Protocols

Isometric Mid-Thigh Pull (IMTP)

IMTP
EquipmentForce plate(s) ≥1000 Hz (or strain gauge 100–133 Hz), immovable bar, straps, goniometer, force-time software.
PersonnelOne tester/recorder.
1. Rack height mirrors weightlifting "2nd pull" position.
2. Knee ≈120–145°, hip ≈125–150°, upright trunk, clean grip + straps.
3. 1–3 familiarization sessions before testing.
4. Submax trials at 50/70/90% → 3+ max trials, 1–2 min rest.
5. Discard trial if pre-tension, countermovement, or unquiet start detected.
Key metrics: peak force and force at 50/100/150/200/250 ms. RFD (peak: 20 ms window) and impulse are quantified over 0–50/0–100/0–200/0–250 ms windows — reported in absolute and relative (per kg) terms.
Topic 03 — Selected Test Protocols

Nordic Hamstring Test

Nordic Hamstring Test
EquipmentInstrumented Nordic device (or video camera in sagittal view on a tripod).
PersonnelOne tester/recorder.
1. Kneeling, ankles under hooks, knees at 90°; no hip flexion to reduce loading.
2. Athlete leans forward under control, extending the knees, straight line knees–shoulders.
3. Hands push back up to start; typically 3–6 sets.
Instrumented: record absolute (N) or scaled eccentric force, torque (Nm), or L/R asymmetry %. Video-only: report the break-point angle — where the athlete loses active control of the descent — as a moderate proxy for eccentric hamstring strength.

Topic 03 — Selected Test Protocols

Measuring the Vertical Jump

Force Plate
EquipmentCalibrated force plate + connected computer/tablet/phone.
PersonnelOne tester/recorder.
1. Zero the plate immediately before each jump.
2. No preparatory/stutter step.
3. Hands on hips (or arm swing = Abalakov) — stay consistent.
Wall & Chalk
EquipmentSmooth wall, chalk (2 colors).
PersonnelOne tester/recorder.
1. Mark standing reach, then jump + mark peak reach.
2. Score = distance between marks.
3. Best of 3, nearest 0.5 in / 1 cm.
Vertec Device
EquipmentCommercial stacked-vane device.
PersonnelOne tester/recorder.
1. Set vane stack to standing reach height.
2. Athlete jumps, taps highest vane.
3. Best of 3, nearest 0.5 in / 1 cm.
Jump-and-reach values (wall/Vertec) measure hand-reach height and are not interchangeable with force-plate/contact-mat values, which measure center-of-mass rise from takeoff.
Topic 03 — Selected Test Protocols

Standing Long Jump & Margaria-Kalamen

Standing Long (Broad) Jump
EquipmentFlat area ≥20 ft; tape measure; tape for start line (or premarked jump mat).
PersonnelOne distance judge, one recorder.
1. Toes behind the start line; countermovement + jump forward.
2. Must land on feet to score.
3. Best of 3, nearest 0.5 in / 1 cm, measured from rearmost heel.
Margaria-Kalamen Test
Equipment9+ step staircase (~7 in/step), 20 ft lead-up, electronic timer (start/stop), scale.
PersonnelOne tester/recorder.
1. Sprint from 20 ft, up the stairs 3-at-a-time (3rd→6th→9th step).
2. Time from 3rd- to 9th-step contact, nearest 0.01 s.
3. Power (W) = (weight in N × height in m) ÷ time; 3 trials, 2–3 min rest.
Topic 03 — Selected Test Protocols

Velocity-Based Testing (VBT)

VBT Protocol
EquipmentCommercial VBT device (IMU, linear position transducer, depth-sensing camera, or smartphone app) + barbell/plates for the chosen lift.
PersonnelOne tester/recorder.
1. Position the device per its type (transducer under/tethered to bar; IMU affixed to bar).
2. Select the exercise per athlete/sport/session needs (e.g. back squat).
3. Record velocity (m/s), power (W), and peak force (N) from the device.
VBT output feeds directly into the load-velocity profile (Topic 01) — useful for prescribing intensity, but not for predicting 1RM.
Topic 03 — Selected Test Protocols

Anaerobic Capacity Tests

300-Yd (274 m) Shuttle
EquipmentStopwatch (0.1 s), two lines 25 yd apart.
PersonnelOne timer, two line judges.
1. 6 × 50 yd round trips, as fast as possible.
2. 5 min rest → second trial.
3. Score = average of 2 trials.
90-s Box Jump
EquipmentStopwatch, box 18 in × 30 in, nonslip surface.
PersonnelOne recorder/tester.
1. Jump box-to-side-to-side for 90 s.
2. Two feet off ground, two feet touch top.
3. Count reps reaching the top with proper form.
Wingate test (cycle ergometer): 30 s all-out against a load proportional to body weight (≈7.5%). Reports peak power, average power, and a fatigue index (max ÷ min interval power).
Topic 03 — Selected Test Protocols

Local Muscular Endurance I

Partial Curl-Up
EquipmentFlat mat/surface, cadence source.
PersonnelOne recorder/technique judge.
1. Preferred over full sit-up — removes hip-flexor contribution.
2. Measures abdominal muscular endurance to a set cadence.
3. Max valid reps to fatigue or 75-rep cap.
Push-Up (Standard/Modified)
Equipment4 in foam roller (modified version only).
PersonnelOne recorder/technique judge.
1. Standard: hands shoulder-width, chest taps recorder's vertical fist at bottom.
2. Modified: knees down at 90°, ankles crossed, chest taps foam roller.
3. Reps not reaching the bottom position don't count.
Topic 03 — Selected Test Protocols

Local Muscular Endurance II

AFT Hand-Release Push-Up
EquipmentLevel, nonslip surface.
PersonnelOne recorder/technique judge.
1. Prone start, chest/hips/legs down, hands flat, index fingers inside shoulders.
2. Max reps in 2 min; straight line ankles-to-head throughout.
3. Hand-lift or arm-extension variant at the bottom; >5 s motionless ends test.
YMCA Bench Press Test
EquipmentBarbell/plates/locks (80 lb men / 35 lb women), flat bench, metronome.
PersonnelOne spotter/recorder.
1. Warm up 5–10 reps light-moderate load.
2. Metronome at 60 bpm = 30 reps/min (1 beat up, 1 beat down).
3. Continue in time until the athlete can no longer keep pace.
Topic 03 — Selected Test Protocols

Aerobic Capacity Tests

1.5-Mile Run
Steady maximal pace over 1.5 mi (2.4 km); time converts to estimated VO₂max via norm tables.
12-Minute Run
Max distance covered in 12 min (laps × 400 m).
Yo-Yo IRT1
2×20 m shuttles at increasing speed + 10 s recovery; starts 10 km/h; ends when pace can't be held for 2 trials.
MAS Test
Speed ↑ 1 km/h every 2 min until failure; last speed held ≥2 min ≈ VO₂max speed; VO₂max ≈ 3.5 × MAS.
30-15 IFT
Shuttle speed ↑ 0.5 km/h each stage between lines 40 m apart, 15 s recovery; stops after 3 missed beeps; distance corrected 0.7 s per change of direction.
Topic 03 — Selected Test Protocols

Agility Tests I

T-Test
Equipment4 cones, tape measure ≥5 yd, stopwatch/timing gates.
PersonnelOne tester/recorder, one spotter.
1. Sprint A→B, shuffle B→C (5 yd), shuffle C→D (10 yd), shuffle D→B (5 yd), backpedal to A.
2. No foot-crossing while shuffling; face forward throughout.
3. Best of 2 trials, nearest 0.1 s.
Hexagon Test
EquipmentContrasting tape, measuring tape/stick, stopwatch.
PersonnelOne timer/recorder, one line judge.
1. 24 in hexagon sides at 120°; athlete starts centered.
2. Double-leg hop out/back over each side clockwise — 3 full revolutions (18 jumps).
3. Landing on a side or losing balance restarts the trial; best of 3, nearest 0.1 s.
Topic 03 — Selected Test Protocols

Agility Tests II

5-10-5 (Pro Agility)
Equipment3 lines 5 yd apart, stopwatch/timing gates.
PersonnelOne timer, one line judge.
1. 3-point stance: 5 yd left, 10 yd right, 5 yd back to center.
2. Hand/foot contact required on every line.
3. Best of 2 trials, nearest 0.01 s.
5-0-5 Test
EquipmentTiming gates/stopwatch, marked run-in + turn line.
PersonnelOne timer/recorder, one line judge.
1. 10 m run-in → 5 m to turn line → sprint back through the 10 m gate.
2. Slow down only after passing the gate the 2nd time.
3. Best of 2, nearest 0.1 s; can test either turning leg.
Illinois Test
EquipmentStopwatch/timing lights, tape measure, 8 cones.
PersonnelOne tester/recorder.
1. 10 m × 5 m course; slalom through 4 center cones both ways.
2. Prone start → sprint → slalom → sprint to finish.
3. Single timed trial.
Topic 03 — Selected Test Protocols

Speed / Sprint Test

Linear Sprint
EquipmentTiming gates (or stopwatch), flat surface, ≥20 yd deceleration zone past the finish.
PersonnelOne timer/recorder.
1. Warm-up + 2 submax practice runs.
2. 3- or 4-point start.
3. Sprint the set distance at max effort.
4. Best of 2 trials, nearest 0.1 s.
Multiple gates at 10/20/40 yd give split times → insight into acceleration vs max-velocity capacities separately, not just a single finish time.
Topic 03 — Selected Test Protocols

Balance Tests

BESS
EquipmentFoam balance pad, stopwatch.
PersonnelOne timer/recorder.
1. 3 stances (double-leg, single-leg, tandem) × firm + soft surface.
2. 20 s each, eyes closed, hands on hips.
3. Errors summed into a single score.
SEBT (Star Excursion)
EquipmentAdhesive tape (8-line star, 120 cm reach lines).
PersonnelOne recorder.
1. Single-leg stance, reach with the other leg along each line.
2. 3 trials per direction, averaged; 15 s rest between reaches.
3. Anteromedial/medial/posteromedial usually sufficient.
Topic 03 — Selected Test Protocols

Flexibility Tests

Sit-and-Reach
EquipmentMeasuring tape/stick (or a standard box), adhesive tape.
PersonnelOne tester/recorder.
1. Warm up hamstrings/low back first (3–5 min).
2. Sit shoeless, feet 12 in apart, heels at the 15 in mark.
3. Reach forward slowly, hold — best of 3, nearest 0.25 in.
Overhead Squat
EquipmentWooden dowel or barbell.
PersonnelOne tester/recorder.
1. Grip = 2× biacromial width, overhead, elbows locked.
2. Squat until hip crease is below the knee, heels down.
3. Qualitative pass/fail, viewed from the side, 5+ reps.
Stay consistent with the device used (tape vs box) across repeat testing of the same athlete — don't switch methods mid-tracking. Ballistic bouncing invalidates any flexibility test.
Topic 03 — Selected Test Protocols

Body Composition Tests

Skinfold Measurements
EquipmentSkinfold calipers, flexible tape measure, marking pen.
PersonnelOne tester, one recorder.
1. Measure dry skin, before exercise, for max validity/reliability.
2. Sites/equations chosen based on the population tested.
3. Most valid/reliable field method available (r = 0.99) — trained tester required.
Bioelectrical Impedance (BIA)
EquipmentBIA device.
PersonnelOne recorder.
1. Program device with sex, weight, height.
2. Hold electrodes or stand barefoot on them, per device type.
3. Best done first thing in the morning, fasted, post-void, for consistent tracking.
Topic 03 — Selected Test Protocols

Anthropometry Testing

Height & Weight
EquipmentStadiometer (or flat wall + tape); certified balance/electronic scale.
PersonnelOne tester/recorder.
1. Height without shoes, nearest ¼ in / ½ cm.
2. Weigh in minimal dry clothing, same time of day for repeat tests.
3. Most reliable: morning, post-void, before food/fluids.
Girth Measurements
EquipmentFlexible spring-loaded tape (e.g. Gulick tape).
PersonnelOne tester, one recorder.
1. Relaxed anatomical position for each site.
2. Chest, upper arm, forearm, waist, hips, thigh, calf.
3. Measure at the start of a training period for future comparison.

Topic 04 — Statistical Evaluation

Why Analyze Test Data?

1
Track change in performance over the training period.
2
Compare an individual/group to similarly tested groups.
3
Relate an athlete's score to the rest of the group.
4
Compare scores to local, state, national, or international norms.
Two traps when judging improvement alone: higher-status athletes improve less (diminishing returns), and athletes may sandbag the pretest to inflate their "improvement." Insist on maximal effort at both ends.
Topic 04 — Statistical Evaluation

Types of Statistics

Descriptive
Summarizes/describes a whole known population.
Central tendency, variability, percentile rank.
Inferential
Draws conclusions about a population from a sample.
Assumes the sample truly represents the population.
Magnitude
Interprets the practical/clinical significance of change.
Smallest worthwhile change, effect size.
Topic 04 — Statistical Evaluation

Central Tendency

Mean
Sum of scores ÷ number of scores.
Most commonly used measure.
Median
Middle score when ranked.
Resists distortion from extreme scores.
Mode
Most frequent score.
Regarded as least useful measure.
Topic 04 — Statistical Evaluation

Variability

Range
Lowest score to highest score.
Simple, but ignores everything in between.
Standard Deviation
Dispersion of scores about the mean.
Small SD = tightly clustered; large SD = spread out.
Standard Deviation (Sample)
SD = √[Σ(x − x̄)² / (n − 1)]
x
Individual score
x̄
Mean of the scores
n
Sample size
Topic 04 — Statistical Evaluation

The Z-Score

Standardized Score
z = (x − x̄) / SD
x
Athlete's score
x̄
Group mean
SD
Group standard deviation
Worked example: 40-yd sprint = 4.6 s. Group mean = 5.00 s, SD = 0.33 s. z = (4.6 − 5.00) / 0.33 = −1.2 → 1.2 SD below (faster than) the group mean.
Plotting z-scores across multiple tests (strength, jump, agility, sprint, flexibility, body fat) visually flags which quality to prioritize in programming.
Topic 04 — Statistical Evaluation

Percentile Rank

Percentile Rank
% of test-takers scoring below a given individual.
Scores are ordered lowest → highest, same logic as the median.
Example75th percentile → 75% of the group scored below that athlete.
Topic 04 — Statistical Evaluation

Magnitude Statistics — SWC

Smallest Worthwhile Change
Smallest practically important change a test can detect.
Typically = 0.2 × between-subjects SD.
ExampleVertical jump SD = 10 cm in a female group → SWC = 0.2 × 10 = 2 cm.
Topic 04 — Statistical Evaluation

Magnitude Statistics — Effect Size

Effect Size
ES = (x̄ post − x̄ pre) / SD pre
x̄ post
Mean score after training
x̄ pre
Mean score before training
SD pre
Pretest standard deviation
Example: Bench 1RM pre = 104.5 kg (SD 5.7). Post (12 wk) = 111.7 kg. ES = (111.7 − 104.5) / 5.7 = 1.26 → interpreted as a large training effect.

Topic 05 — Developing an Athletic Profile

What Is an Athletic Profile?

Athletic Profile
A group of test results tied to the sport-specific abilities that matter for quality performance in a sport or position.
Built by combining selected test results, not any single score.
Topic 05 — Developing an Athletic Profile

The 6-Step Process

1
Select tests measuring parameters tied to the sport's physical demands.
2
Choose valid/reliable tests; sequence the battery with sufficient rest.
3
Administer the battery to as many athletes as possible.
4
Determine smallest worthwhile change; compare to norms where available.
5
Repeat testing (pre/post) and visualize the profile.
6
Use results to identify strengths/weaknesses and guide programming.
Topic 05 — Developing an Athletic Profile

Applied Example — Wrestling

Building a Wrestling Battery
Sport demands pulling strength, pushing strength, local muscular endurance.
Battery: max-rep push-ups + max-rep sit-ups.
Sequencing ruleSeparate the two tests by ≥10 min rest to allow recovery and protect score accuracy.
It is recommended that coaches store testing results over time and build their own norms once standardized procedures are in place.
