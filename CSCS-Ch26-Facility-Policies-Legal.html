Topic 03 — Legal & Ethical Issues

Informed Consent & Liability

Informed Consent
Procedure/activity described to participant.
Inherent risks AND benefits explained.
Individual decides whether to participate.
Liability
A legal responsibility, duty or obligation.
Duty to athletes by virtue of employment: act when injury occurs and prevent it.
Topic 03 — Legal & Ethical Issues

Standard of Care & Assumption of Risk

Standard of Care
What a reasonable & prudent person would do in similar circumstances.
Held to education, level of training & certification (CSCS, NSCA-CPT, EMT, CPR, AED, first aid).
Assumption of Risk
Knowing inherent risk exists + voluntarily participating anyway.
Athletes informed & should sign a statement.
Limit
An assumption-of-risk waiver may not protect from liability if the professional is found negligent.
Topic 03 — Legal & Ethical Issues

Negligence: Four Elements

All four must exist for a S&C professional to be found negligent.

1
Duty
A duty to act existed
2
Breach of Duty
Failed to act at the standard of care
3
Proximate Cause
Harm from natural, continuous sequence of reasonably foreseeable events
4
Damages
Physical or economic injury to another person
Topic 03 — Legal & Ethical Issues

Negligence Example

Scenario
Pro sees a cable excessively worn on a machine — but posts no "Out of Order" sign.
Athlete uses it → injured.
Why Negligent
Duty: fix cable or post sign.
Breach: did neither.
Proximate cause + damages: injury from a foreseeable break.

Topic 03 — Legal & Ethical Issues

Negligence in Court

Tort & Civil Law
Tort = a civil wrong.
Civil law = private rights & remedies via suit — distinct from criminal proceedings.
Burden of Proof
Preponderance of the evidence = greater weight of evidence.
Each element (duty, breach, causation, damages) must be proven.
Damages
Money claimed by, or ordered paid to, a person as compensation for loss or injury.
Topic 03 — Legal & Ethical Issues

Case: Exertional Rhabdomyolysis

Facts
Filed Jan 9, 2019 — Circuit Court, Oregon.
Former student-athlete: "non-evidence based physical punishment regimens" → permanent renal injury.
Lesson
Programs without evidence-based foundation → exertional rhabdo (can mean permanent kidney damage).
Standard of care = evidence-based, research-supported programming.
Topic 03 — Legal & Ethical Issues

Case: Sickle Cell Trait

Facts
Pro knew (or should have known) athlete had sickle cell trait.
Same work-to-rest ratio as athletes without it → organ damage or death.
Jury found respondent liable; damages awarded.
Lesson
Duty: train within parameters protecting the athlete's health.
Modify work-to-rest ratio for diagnosed conditions.
Failure to act = breach → foreseeable harm.
Topic 03 — Legal & Ethical Issues

How Cases End

Resolution
Jury trial.
Settlement before court.
Dismissal by judge via summary judgment — no genuine issue of material fact; movant wins as matter of law.
Sovereign Immunity
Government's immunity from suit in its own courts without consent.
State's immunity from federal suit by its own citizens.
Can shield state defendants.
Prejudice
With prejudice: plaintiff barred from refiling same claim.
Without prejudice: may refile within limitations period.
Topic 03 — Legal & Ethical Issues

Case: State University

Facts
US District Court, N. District of Mississippi (Oxford).
Federal & state claims vs state university + head football coach.
Official capacity: Title VI, Title IX, ADA, Rehab Act, gross negligence, negligence.
Individual capacity: equal protection; intentional infliction of emotional distress.
Outcome
Motion for summary judgment granted.
Lesson: consistently demonstrate an evidence-based, research-supported standard of care.
Topic 03 — Legal & Ethical Issues

HIPAA & Equal Treatment

HIPAA
Use medical-evaluation info while staying HIPAA compliant.
Ensure athletes with significant conditions (sickle cell trait, prior heat illness, asthma) get proper work-to-rest ratios.
Title VI & Title IX
Title VI → racial discrimination claims; Title IX → sex discrimination claims.
Benefits to each team must be equal and non-discriminatory by sex.
Women's vs Men's Sports — Equal Basis
Workout times · program design · equipment availability · technology assessments & evaluations (strength, power, body comp) · nutritional supplements.

Definition
Time during which an individual can file a lawsuit.

Topic 05 — Record Keeping, Insurance & Product Liability

Liability Insurance

Definition
Contractual promise: insurer defends & indemnifies insured, up to defined limits, from certain defined liability risks — at insurer's cost, for a premium.
Staff Should
Buy professional liability insurance — especially if not covered by facility policy.
Consult HR manager, legal consultant or professional organization (e.g. NSCA).
Topic 05 — Record Keeping, Insurance & Product Liability

Product Liability

Definition
Legal responsibility of those who manufacture or sell products if a person is injured using them.
S&C pros usually don't sell — but can be codefendants.
Two Key Considerations
Was the product changed from its original condition?
Was it used as the manufacturer intended?
Fail either → responsibility shifts to the S&C professional.
Topic 05 — Record Keeping, Insurance & Product Liability

Avoiding Product Liability I

Use & Standards
Use equipment only as the manufacturer intended (age/size specs).
Meet professional standards; avoid equipment deemed unsafe; heed recalls — return immediately.
Buy & Modify
Buy only from reputable manufacturers (check safety record, claims — BBB, colleagues, organizations).
Don't modify unless instructions clearly designate it; follow exactly.
Topic 05 — Record Keeping, Insurance & Product Liability

Avoiding Product Liability II

Warnings & Inspection
Apply all warning labels in plain view — or be held liable.
Continually inspect for damage/wear; inspect new equipment before use.
Damaged on arrival → notify manufacturer/seller, get replacement.
Damaged & Supervised
Owned equipment damaged → remove for repair/replacement; if too large, affix a "do not use" sign.
Never allow unsupervised athletes on equipment.

Supplements, Ergogenic Aids & Banned Substances

Topic 07 — Instruction & Supervision

Staff-to-Athlete Ratios (Guideline 3.1)

Guideline
Plan so qualified staff meet minimums at peak usage:
100 ft² per participant · participants per barbell / station · professional-to-participant ratio.
Ratios
1:10 junior high
1:15 high school
1:20 college
Modify
↑ supervision when exercises involve complex movements.
Topic 07 — Instruction & Supervision

Why Supervision Matters

Litigation
Supervision cited in ~80% of court cases involving athletic injury.
Inadequate instruction/supervision is often a main cause.
Practice
Constant supervision — physical presence of a qualified pro.
Supervisor stations with clear view of facility & athletes.
Ratios vary: machine circuit < free weights; low training age → ↑ supervision.
Performance & Travel
Strength gains ↑ with lower supervision ratios.
Optimal ratio not possible? Keep working toward it.
Attend practices/competitions/travel only if it doesn't break facility coach-to-athlete ratio.

Topic 08 — Facility Administration & Staff Policies

Facility Rules I: Safety

Before & During
Screening & clearance first; orientation on risks, technique, consequences.
Have & record a training program.
Injured athlete → modified program from sports medicine.
Spotters for bar on back/front shoulders or over face/head. Power exercises are not spotted.
Equipment
Locks on all barbell ends.
No squatting outside the rack.
Bumper plates on platforms for power exercises.
No belts contacting upholstery.
Move weights rack→bar only; no plates on floor/leaned; return dumbbells in order; no dropping/throwing.

Topic 09 — Emergency Planning & Response

Emergency Protocol

Fire
1. Activate fire alarm.
2. Evacuate the building.
Life-Threatening
1. Call 911.
2. Don't move victim.
3. If conscious, ask permission for first aid.
4. CPR/AED if needed.
5. Stay until help arrives.
6. Communication plan.
7. Injury report.
Environmental
1. Alarm; safe location.
2. Call 911 if needed.
3. Account for all.
4. Communication plan.
5. Injury report.
Non-Life-Threatening
1. First aid.
2. Call 911 if necessary.
3. Communication plan.
4. Injury report.
